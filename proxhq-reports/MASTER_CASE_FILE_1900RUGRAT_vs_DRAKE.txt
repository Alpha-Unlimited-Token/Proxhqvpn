  WHAT THE NUMBERS SAY:

  1. IOI PATTERN 0.9988 — MOST IMPORTANT FINDING
     The inter-onset interval distribution is virtually identical.
     This captures the GROOVE TEMPLATE — the spacing and feel of beats,
     kicks, hats, and rhythmic pulses across the song. A score of 0.9988
     means these songs have essentially the same rhythmic DNA, regardless
     of tempo or key. This is consistent with one beat being derived from
     or inspired by the other.

  2. MFCC 0.9848 — VERY IMPORTANT
     The timbral fingerprint (the "sound" of the production — synthesizer
     character, reverb signature, compression style, saturation) is nearly
     identical. This goes beyond genre similarity. Scores above 0.98 are
     rarely coincidental between two different independent productions.

  3. SPECTRAL CONTRAST 0.9994 — VERY IMPORTANT
     The distribution of energy across frequency bands is virtually
     identical. This captures how the producer balanced lows, mids, and
     highs — a production "EQ fingerprint." Again, 0.9994 exceeds normal
     same-genre similarity.

  4. CHROMA AT +6 SEMITONES = 0.9545
     After transposing Rugrat's track up 6 semitones (or Drake's down 6),
     the harmonic content matches strongly. This 6-semitone (tritone)
     shift is unusual — producers who deliberately pitch-shift a reference
     beat often choose exactly this interval to "hide" the similarity.

     The BPM difference is significant BUT does not preclude infringement.
     Producers routinely time-stretch beats. A common technique is to take
     a fast trap beat and time-stretch it to a slower BPM (in this case,
     roughly 63% of original tempo) to create a more melodic/atmospheric
     feel. This is a standard production technique at major labels.
