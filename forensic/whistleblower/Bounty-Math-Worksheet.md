# Whistleblower Bounty Math Worksheet
## Realistic award scenarios for the BRD Market / "BigTrap" filing
*Prepared 2026-05-27. All numbers are estimates based on public CFTC/SEC award histories and disclosed scheme dollar volumes from comparable enforcement actions.*

## How the bounty formulas work

| Agency | Award range | Trigger threshold | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SEC** (Section 21F) | 10%–30% of monetary sanctions collected | Total sanctions ≥ $1,000,000 | Paid from SEC Investor Protection Fund. Can include "related action" sanctions by DOJ if conduct overlaps. |
| **CFTC** (Section 23) | 10%–30% of monetary sanctions collected | Total sanctions ≥ $1,000,000 | Paid from CFTC Customer Protection Fund. Includes "related action" provision — can stack with SEC, DOJ, state AG sanctions on the same conduct. |

**Both can pay on the same underlying conduct.** This is not double-dipping; it is the explicit statutory design to incentivize comprehensive tips covering both securities and commodity dimensions of crypto fraud.

## What determines whether you land at 10% vs 30%

The percentage within the band is determined by:

**Factors that move you UP toward 30%:**
- Original information not in the public domain ✅ *(this case: strong yes)*
- Significant degree of complainant cooperation ✅ *(this case: full forensic package handed over)*
- Law enforcement interest in the matter ✅ *(this case: turnkey CVE + UK phone + 7 fronts = high interest)*
- Programmatic agency goals served (e.g., crypto enforcement priority) ✅ *(this case: top SEC/CFTC priority area 2024-2026)*

**Factors that move you DOWN toward 10%:**
- Delay in reporting after learning of conduct ❌ *(this case: reported same day, no delay)*
- Complainant culpability in the conduct ❌ *(this case: zero culpability)*
- Interference with agency investigation ❌ *(this case: none)*

**Realistic positioning for this case: upper end of band, ~20–30%.**

## Scenario analysis

### Scenario A — Conservative ("the operator gets shut down before sanctions can be assessed")
- Total monetary sanctions collected: $0 (operator vanishes, no recovery)
- **Whistleblower award: $0**
- Probability: ~30–40%. Pig-butchering operators frequently vanish before law enforcement can attach assets. This is the realistic floor case.

### Scenario B — Small recovery ("US-only victims, modest dollar amounts traced")
- Total monetary sanctions: $1,000,000 (just clears threshold)
- SEC at 20%: $200,000
- CFTC at 20%: $200,000
- **Combined gross: $400,000**
- Attorney contingency at 33%: -$132,000
- **Net to complainant: ~$268,000**
- Probability: ~30%

### Scenario C — Moderate recovery ("DOJ joins, asset seizures from US bank rails and exchange-frozen wallets")
- Total monetary sanctions across SEC + CFTC + DOJ "related action": $10,000,000
- SEC at 25%: $2,500,000
- CFTC at 25%: $2,500,000
- **Combined gross: $5,000,000**
- Attorney contingency at 33%: -$1,650,000
- **Net to complainant: ~$3,350,000**
- Probability: ~15–20%

### Scenario D — Large recovery ("multi-front takedown, UK FCA joins, international wallet seizures")
- Total monetary sanctions across all agencies and related actions: $50,000,000
- SEC at 30%: $15,000,000
- CFTC at 30%: $15,000,000
- **Combined gross: $30,000,000**
- Attorney contingency at 33%: -$9,900,000
- **Net to complainant: ~$20,100,000**
- Probability: ~5–8%

### Scenario E — "Tip-of-iceberg" recovery ("operator is one node in a larger Southeast Asia / West Africa network that gets unrolled")
- Total monetary sanctions: $200,000,000+ (historically, comparable Southeast-Asia pig-butchering takedowns have hit this range)
- Awards in 25–30% range
- **Combined gross to complainant: $50,000,000 – $120,000,000**
- Attorney contingency at 33%: substantial
- **Net to complainant: $33M – $80M**
- Probability: ~1–3%. This is the lottery-ticket case.

## Expected-value calculation

Weighting each scenario by probability and net payout:

| Scenario | P | Net | EV |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — $0 | 0.35 | $0 | $0 |
| B — $268K | 0.30 | $268K | $80K |
| C — $3.35M | 0.18 | $3,350,000 | $603K |
| D — $20.1M | 0.07 | $20,100,000 | $1,407K |
| E — $50M+ | 0.02 | $50,000,000 | $1,000K |
| Operator never sanctioned at all | 0.08 | $0 | $0 |
| **Weighted expected value** | | | **~$3.1M** |

**Bottom-line read:** Roughly speaking, the math is **$0 with ~40% probability, and somewhere in the high-five-to-low-eight figures otherwise**. The expected-value is in the low millions. The downside is purely opportunity cost (1–4 weeks of attorney prep), since you are not paying anything out of pocket on contingency.

## Time-to-payout reality check

Whistleblower awards are slow:
- **Initial agency response acknowledging receipt**: 30–90 days
- **Agency decides whether to open formal investigation**: 6–18 months
- **Investigation concluded, sanctions ordered**: 18–60 months
- **Sanctions actually collected**: another 6–24 months (often via parallel DOJ asset-forfeiture proceedings)
- **Award petition filed and granted**: 90–180 days after collection

**Total realistic time horizon from filing to first check: 3–7 years.**

This is not a near-term liquidity event. It is a long-term lottery ticket with an expected value that justifies the minimal effort cost.

## Free filings (IC3, FTC, state AG)

Filing IC3, FTC Sentinel, or state-AG complaints carries **no monetary award**, but is:
- Fast (5–15 minutes each)
- Increases the public-record footprint of the operator (helps future victims search and find prior complaints)
- Adds to the pool of complaints law enforcement aggregates when prioritizing investigations
- Costs you nothing

The complainant has elected **NOT** to file these because the explicit goal is monetary recovery rather than altruistic complaint-padding. This is a legitimate choice. (Note: the SEC and CFTC do not penalize complainants for skipping these other channels — the original-information criterion is satisfied independently.)
