## Section C — Type of violation alleged

**Commodity Exchange Act violations**, specifically:

### Primary violations
1. **Fraud in connection with the sale of commodities — 7 U.S.C. § 9(1) / CFTC Rule 180.1**
   Cryptocurrencies (specifically Bitcoin and Ether) are commodities subject to CFTC anti-fraud and anti-manipulation jurisdiction (*CFTC v. McDonnell*, 287 F. Supp. 3d 213 (E.D.N.Y. 2018); *CFTC v. My Big Coin Pay, Inc.*, 334 F. Supp. 3d 492 (D. Mass. 2018)). The operator's solicitation of cryptocurrency deposits under materially false pretenses regarding expected returns, regulatory status, and corporate identity constitutes fraud in connection with the sale of commodities.

2. **Operating an unregistered commodity pool — 7 U.S.C. § 6m, CFTC Rules 4.20 and 4.23**
   The marketed structure ("pool client funds, professional team manages, distribute returns") meets the statutory definition of a **commodity pool** under 7 U.S.C. § 1a(10). The operator has made no filing as a Commodity Pool Operator (CPO) or Commodity Trading Advisor (CTA) with either the CFTC or the National Futures Association.

3. **Acting as an unregistered Introducing Broker / Futures Commission Merchant — 7 U.S.C. § 6d**
   By soliciting deposits and purporting to execute or arrange execution of leveraged or margined commodity transactions on behalf of retail investors, the operator is acting as an unregistered IB / FCM.

4. **Solicitation of leveraged retail commodity transactions in violation of CEA § 2(c)(2)(D)**
   Pig-butchering platforms in this template family routinely market "leveraged crypto trading" to retail customers; such transactions, when offered to non-eligible-contract-participants and not resulting in actual delivery within 28 days, fall under §2(c)(2)(D) and require execution on a registered DCM or SEF.

### Why CFTC jurisdiction is independent of SEC jurisdiction
The same conduct may be charged by both agencies because:
- The **offer/sale of the investment product** is an unregistered securities offering (SEC)
- The **underlying instruments deposited and traded** (BTC, ETH, USDT etc.) are commodities under CFTC anti-fraud jurisdiction (CFTC)
- The **commodity-pool structure** is a CFTC-specific statutory violation with no SEC counterpart

Filing both is not duplicative; it is the appropriate enforcement matching for hybrid crypto fraud and is encouraged by the agencies' overlapping enforcement memoranda.

## Section E — Materiality threshold for CFTC whistleblower award

CFTC whistleblower awards are available where the enforcement action results in monetary sanctions **exceeding $1,000,000**. The estimates set out in the SEC Section E (estimated hundreds to low thousands of victims across the affiliated front network, baseline scheme volume of at least $250,000 and realistic volume in the $2.5M – $25M+ range) easily satisfy this threshold.

Additionally, the CFTC whistleblower statute permits awards based on related enforcement actions by other authorities (the "related action" provision, 7 U.S.C. § 26(b)(1)(B)), meaning that recoveries by the SEC, DOJ, state attorneys general, the UK FCA, or NCA against the same operator can additively contribute to the complainant's award pool.
